## Intended main structure

I. Introduction: contribution, causal claim, and roadmap.
II. What the genetic model assumes and predicts: estimands, environmental exclusions, mating mechanism, inheritance, predictions.
III. From observed correlations to genetic parameters: measurement, identification under alternative mating models, binary outcomes, empirical diagnostics.
IV. Good fit, restrictions, and causal identification: historical precedent, omitted covariance, correctly specified versus misspecified estimation.
V. What the environmental comparisons establish: regression to the mean, parental loss and wealth shocks, schooling and within-family comparisons.
VI. What modern genomic evidence can identify: segregation, population versus family PGIs, covariance versus dominance, independent tests.
VII. From marital assortment to historical explanations: variance arithmetic, empirical assortment, cousin marriage, innovation.
VIII. Interpreting genetic estimates and policy claims: Jencks, Goldberger, fair treatment of concessions, educational spending, meritocracy and extrapolation.
IX. Conclusion: contribution and what remains unestablished.

Technical appendices retain proofs, measurement derivations, binary simulation details, restriction/misspecification experiments, and the full balanced quotation dossier. The unfiltered working list and replaced drafts are retained separately in an editorial holding document and original snapshot.
